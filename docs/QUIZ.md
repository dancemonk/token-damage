# The AI Damage Quiz — content

Five questions, real numbers, sources shown after each answer. The point: some AI costs are tiny, some are
much bigger than people think, and *how* you use AI matters more than whether you use it. Never tell anyone
what to do. Mockup: `design/canvas/Quiz.dc.html`.

| # | Question | Options | Answer | Explanation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One chatbot question uses about as much electricity as a microwave running for… | 1 second / 1 minute / 10 minutes | **1 second** | Google measured 0.24 Wh for a typical Gemini text prompt. A microwave uses that in under a second. Chatting is cheap. | Google, Aug 2025 |
| 2 | One heavy coding-agent prompt uses as much energy as how many chatbot questions? | 6 / 60 / 600 | **600** | Coding agents re-read their whole context thousands of times. One researcher's Claude Code logs came to ~150 Wh per prompt, about 9 minutes of microwave. Range 250–1,200. | Hausfather, The Climate Brink, Aug 2026 |
| 3 | How much did RAM contract prices go up in the first 3 months of 2026? | +10% / +45% / +95% | **+95%** | AI data centers need huge amounts of memory, so chipmakers moved production to AI chips. Everyone else pays more. Another +58–63% was expected the next quarter. ✶ Your share of this: +$0.0000079 per stick. Made up. The +95% is real. | TrendForce, Jun 2026 (+93–98% QoQ) |
| 4 | How much water does one chatbot question use? | About 5 drops / About a bottle / Depends who's counting | **Depends who's counting** | Google says ~5 drops (0.26 mL), counting only cooling inside the data center. Researchers who also count the water used by power plants get much bigger numbers. Both measure something real. | Google, Aug 2025 · UC Riverside (Ren) |
| 5 | What share of US electricity will data centers use by 2030? | 3% / 12% / 30% | **12%** | Data centers used 4.4% in 2023. Berkeley Lab now projects about 12% by 2030 (range 9.5–15%). | LBNL 2025 update, Jun 2026 |

Some are not."); 2–3 **MILDLY INFORMED** ("Better than most. The real numbers are weirder than the
headlines."); 4–5 **KNOWS TOO MUCH** ("You know too much. Please touch grass.").
